OpenSearch vs OpenSearch Serverless

Same engine, two very different ways to run it — like renting a whole restaurant kitchen versus hailing a ride. Here's which one your AI app should sit on, explained like you're five.

Step 0

Why does Gen AI even care about OpenSearch?

A large language model is like a brilliant intern with amnesia: smart, but it only knows what's in its head (its training data) and it hallucinates when unsure. RAG fixes that by letting it "look things up" in your documents first. OpenSearch is the filing cabinet it looks things up in — except the documents are stored as vectors (lists of numbers representing meaning), so searching finds things by meaning, not just keywords.

1

You feed it your documents

PDFs, wikis, tickets… each gets chopped into chunks and turned into embeddings (vectors) by a model like Amazon Titan Embeddings.

2

OpenSearch stores the vectors

It builds a k-NN index (usually HNSW) so "find chunks that mean roughly this" takes milliseconds, even across millions of chunks.

3

At question time, retrieve then answer

The user's question is embedded too, the nearest chunks are fetched, and the model answers using those — with citations. Amazon Bedrock Knowledge Bases orchestrates exactly this, and OpenSearch can be its vector store.

The punchline: both "OpenSearch Service" and "OpenSearch Serverless" do this vector-search job well. The real question is how you run the thing — and that's what the rest of this page is about.

The contenders

Two options, one job

Under the hood it's the same search engine. The difference is who worries about servers.

Classic

Amazon OpenSearch Service
(provisioned clusters)

You rent a fleet of servers and shape them yourself.

  • You pick instance types, node counts, shards, replicas
  • Pay per instance-hour — use Reserved Instances for big discounts
  • You (or CloudWatch alarms + auto-scaling) handle growth
  • You own upgrades, JVM tuning, shard planning, snapshots
  • Tiers like UltraWarm / cold storage for old, rarely-read data
ELI5: you lease a fixed-size kitchen and staff it 24/7. Busy or empty, you pay the same. Max control, max responsibility.
Managed

OpenSearch Serverless
(collections, no servers to touch)

AWS runs invisible capacity that grows and shrinks for you.

  • Create a "collection", load data, done — no nodes to size
  • Pay for OpenSearch Compute Units (OCUs) kept warm + storage
  • Autoscales with traffic, including down when it's quiet
  • AWS handles ops, patching, and availability for you
  • Access control via simple data access policies
ELI5: like Uber. Cars (capacity) appear when you need them and vanish when you don't. You pay per ride — but there's a minimum fare.
The ELI5 story

The restaurant kitchen vs the ghost kitchen

Imagine your AI app is a food truck, and the "kitchen" is where your document vectors live.

👨‍🍳

OpenSearch Service = your own kitchen

You lease a kitchen with exactly the ovens you chose. Sunday rush or 3 a.m. quiet, the rent is identical. If your diner traffic is steady, this is cheap and predictable — you can even sign a long lease (Reserved Instances) for a fat discount. But if traffic spikes, your ovens are maxed and customers wait; if it's dead, you're still paying. And you're the one fixing the fridge.

VS
🛵

Serverless = the ghost kitchen

You never see a kitchen. You send orders; capacity appears. Lunchtime rush? It doubles. Midnight? It shrinks. You pay for what gets used — brilliant for spiky, unpredictable, or just-starting-out traffic. The catch: there's a minimum monthly bill (a few compute units run even when nobody orders), so at huge, steady, 24/7 load it can cost more than your own kitchen.

Fine print

Gotchas worth knowing

⚠️ Serverless has a floor

You pay for minimum OCUs even when nothing is happening. A forgotten dev collection quietly bills forever. Delete what you don't use.

⚠️ Provisioned needs a planner

Undersize shards and your RAG app gets slow at the worst time; oversize and you burn money. Vector indexes (HNSW) are memory-hungry — memory-optimized instances are the usual pick.

✅ Match dims to your model

Vector size must match your embedding model (e.g., Titan Embeddings vs OpenAI differ). Changing models means re-embedding everything — pick early.

TL;DR

Explain it to me in four lines

  • Both are the same engine for storing & searching your GenAI knowledge as vectors — great for RAG.
  • Provisioned = your own kitchen: cheapest and most controllable at steady, heavy, 24/7 load — if someone on your team enjoys tuning clusters.
  • Serverless = hailing a ride: zero ops and perfect for spiky or unknown traffic — with a minimum bill that stings at large steady scale.
  • Starting a GenAI project and unsure? Start Serverless, watch the bill, and graduate to provisioned if your workload becomes big and boringly steady.
