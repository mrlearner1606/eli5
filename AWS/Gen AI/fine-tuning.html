Fine-Tuning, explained simply

What it means to take a general-purpose AI model and re-train it on your examples — and how that differs from RAG, continued pre-training, and distillation on AWS.

The one-line version

ELI5

Fine-tuning is sending a smart intern to bootcamp for your specific job. The model already knows how to read, write, and reason — you're just drilling it on your way of doing things until it gets them right, every time, without reminders.

A foundation model (like Amazon Nova or Meta Llama on Amazon Bedrock) was trained on a huge slice of the internet. That makes it a brilliant generalist — but a generalist doesn't know your company's tone, your JSON output format, or how your support tickets should be classified. Fine-tuning continues the model's training on a small, carefully chosen set of your examples, nudging its internal settings ("weights") toward your task.

Why does it exist?

🎭

Prompting only steers so far

You can beg a model in the prompt to "always answer in strict JSON with these fields" — and it will obey mostly. Fine-tuning bakes the behavior into the model, so you stop paying the tax of long instructions on every call.

🎯

Quality on one task beats quality on all tasks

A base model is a jack of all trades. Fine-tuned on 1,000 great examples of your task, a smaller, cheaper model can outperform the giant generalist — at lower cost and latency.

🧠

Behavior, not encyclopedias

Fine-tuning is for teaching style, format, and judgment. It is famously bad at reliably storing fresh facts — that's RAG's job (more on that below).

The kitchen analogy

Imagine you run a restaurant and you hire a world-class chef who has cooked everything, everywhere. Three ways to get the dishes you want:

🗣️

Prompting — shout instructions

"Make it less salty, plate it our way, use our garnish!" Works… until the dinner rush, when the chef occasionally forgets. Cheap, instant, fragile.

📚

RAG — hand them a recipe binder

Before each order, the chef looks up your recipe book (your documents, searched at request time). Great for facts that change — today's menu, prices, policy.

🎓

Fine-tuning — culinary bootcamp

The chef spends a week practicing on your recipe cards until your style becomes muscle memory. No reminders needed — it's baked in. That's fine-tuning.

Real systems often combine them: a fine-tuned model (your house style) + RAG (today's facts) + a short prompt (tonight's special).

How it works — five steps

  1. Gather examples Write input→ideal-output pairs: "When a customer asks X, the perfect answer is Y." On Amazon Bedrock these live as JSONL files — one JSON object per line — using a prompt/completion format or the Converse message format for chat models.
  2. Split into training + validation Most rows go to training; a held-out slice goes to validation so you can see whether the model actually learned the task or just memorized the answers. Bedrock accepts both files in S3.
  3. Pick a fine-tunable base model Not every model can be fine-tuned. On Bedrock the eligible list includes Amazon Nova models, Meta Llama 3.x, Titan image models, and Anthropic Claude 3 Haiku — the list changes, so check the docs before you design around one.
  4. Run the training job You set a few knobs — epochs (how many passes over the data), learning rate (how big each correction step is), batch size — and AWS runs the compute. You don't rent or manage GPUs yourself.
  5. Evaluate, deploy, invoke Compare outputs against your validation set. If it's good, deploy the custom model and call it through its own model ARN — your app code barely changes, just the model ID it points at.

Fine-tuning vs everything else

The most common mistake in Gen AI architecture is choosing fine-tuning when you needed one of these instead:

Comparison of model adaptation options
Approach What it teaches Reach for it when… On AWS
Prompting Nothing — instructions per call Starting out; the task changes often No training
RAG Facts, at question time Knowledge is large, private, or changes often Bedrock Knowledge Bases
Fine-tuning Style, format, task behavior Outputs must be consistent and you have good examples Bedrock Fine-tuning
Continued pre-training A whole domain's language Deep specialized vocabulary (legal, medical, proprietary code) Bedrock CPT
Distillation A small model to imitate a big one You need a big model's quality at a small model's cost/latency Bedrock Model Distillation
Reinforcement fine-tuning Behavior from graded feedback You can score answers automatically; newer capability Bedrock RFT

Rule of thumb: facts → RAG, behavior → fine-tuning, domain language → continued pre-training, cost → distillation. When in doubt, start with prompting + RAG and reach for fine-tuning once you've collected a few hundred high-quality examples.

Fine-tuning on Amazon Bedrock

🧩

Which models

Eligibility varies by model and region — Amazon Nova and Titan, Meta Llama 3.x, and Claude 3 Haiku have been on the list. Newer top-tier models often aren't fine-tunable; check the supported-models page for what's live today.

🗂️

Data format

JSONL: one example per line. Text tasks use {"prompt": ..., "completion": ...}; chat models use the Converse-style messages format with system/user/assistant turns. Minimum record counts vary by model (tens to thousands).

💰

Cost shape

You pay three ways: for training (per model's unit — tokens or time, depending on model), a small monthly storage fee per custom model, and inference — historically via Provisioned Throughput, with on-demand-style custom model deployments now also available. Check current pricing before committing.

🔒

Where your data lives

Training data goes to S3 in your account, and AWS states customer content isn't used to train the underlying base models. The custom model belongs to your account — nobody else can invoke it.

Gotchas · the fine print

Garbage in, gourmet garbage out

500 clean, diverse, correctly-formatted examples beat 10,000 sloppy ones. The model learns your typos just as eagerly as your intent. Budget most of your effort here.

It teaches behavior, not news

Fine-tuned models are unreliable at recalling specific facts — they interpolate, they don't memorize reliably. Anything that changes (prices, policy, product docs) belongs in RAG.

Overfitting & catastrophic forgetting

Too many epochs and the model parrots your training set and gets worse at everything else. Watch the validation metrics and stop before it memorizes.

Custom ≠ cheap to host

Invoking your custom model has its own pricing separate from on-demand base models — historically via always-on Provisioned Throughput, billed hourly per model unit. Know the number before you ship.

Not every model, not every region

Fine-tuning availability is model- and region-specific, and the roster shifts as models launch. Design with the docs open, not from memory.

You froze a snapshot

Your custom model is based on one version of a base model. When the provider ships a better base, your fine-tune doesn't auto-upgrade — plan to re-train periodically.

TL;DR

  • Fine-tuning = continue training a foundation model on your own input→output examples so the behavior becomes permanent.
  • Use it for style, format, and task consistency — not for fresh facts (that's RAG) and not for whole new domains (that's continued pre-training).
  • On Amazon Bedrock: JSONL data in S3 → pick an eligible model (Nova, Llama 3.x, Titan, Claude 3 Haiku…) → set epochs / learning rate / batch size → evaluate → deploy and invoke via the custom model ARN.
  • Costs arrive three ways: training + monthly storage + inference — verify current numbers on the pricing page.
  • Start with prompting + RAG; fine-tune once you've banked a few hundred high-quality examples.
