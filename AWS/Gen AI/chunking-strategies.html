Chunking Strategies

Your AI can only answer from the snippets you feed it. How you cut your documents into snippets decides whether RAG finds wisdom or confetti. Explained like you're five, with a pair of scissors and a library.

AWS Bedrock Knowledge Bases RAG · retrieval quality Embeddings · vector stores
Step 0

Why does Gen AI care how we cut documents?

Imagine turning your whole library into index cards so a librarian can find the right card in milliseconds. That's chunking: cutting documents into pieces, turning each piece into a vector (an embedding), and storing them in a vector store. When someone asks a question, the librarian fetches the most similar cards and your LLM answers from those. Three constraints make the cutting matter:

1

Models can only read so much

Embedding models cap input length (a few thousand tokens), and the final prompt must fit the LLM's context window. You physically can't stuff a 200-page PDF into one shot.

2

Retrieval matches meanings, not chapters

A chunk's embedding represents that chunk's meaning. A 20-page chunk about ten topics matches nothing well; a one-line fragment matches tightly but says nothing.

3

The LLM answers only from what it's given

Top-k chunks are your model's entire world for that answer. Cut the right fact in half across two chunks and retrieval may surface neither half completely.

The punchline: RAG quality is decided at the scissors, not at the model. The fanciest LLM can't rescue badly cut chunks.

The contenders

Six ways to hold the scissors

From dumb-but-predictable to smart-but-expensive. Most production systems start simple and upgrade only where retrieval measurably fails.

📏

Fixed-size

Chop every N tokens, done.

Every chunk is the same size (say 300 tokens), often with an overlap so boundary ideas aren't lost. Uniform, predictable, cheap.

✅ Best for: uniform text, quick baselines, when you mostly care about cost math. ⚠️ Watch: cuts mid-sentence and mid-idea without overlap — see the demo above.
📖

Recursive / sentence-aware

Split where the text has seams.

Try paragraphs first, then sentences, then words — until pieces fit the target size. The text's own structure does the cutting. This is the classic default (LangChain's RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter, Bedrock KB's default).

✅ Best for: most prose — reports, wikis, articles. Start here. ⚠️ Watch: one giant run-on paragraph still becomes one giant chunk.
🧠

Semantic

Split where the meaning shifts.

Embed each sentence, then cut wherever similarity between neighbors drops off — so "battery spec" and "review complaints" don't share a card even though they share a paragraph.

✅ Best for: docs that hop between topics; retrieval quality is worth extra compute. ⚠️ Watch: needs an extra embedding pass; tuning the breakpoint is fiddly.
🧱

Hierarchical (parent-child)

Search the small card, return the whole page.

Cut tiny children for precise matching, but each child remembers its big parent section. Retrieval hits the child; the LLM receives the parent — precision and context.

✅ Best for: contracts, legal and medical text where fragments aren't enough. ⚠️ Watch: bigger prompts (parents are large); more config to tune.
🧾

Layout / document-aware

Respect headings, tables, and code.

Parse the document's real structure: split on headings, keep each table or code block whole, attach Q&A pairs together. Chunking that knows it's reading a PDF, not a text stream.

✅ Best for: manuals, financial tables, technical docs, codebases. ⚠️ Watch: needs parsers; messy scanned PDFs will fight you.
🏷️

Contextual enrichment

Every chunk carries its address.

Prepend context to each chunk: document title, section path ("Warranty > Refunds > EU"), or even a model-written summary of where the chunk sits — so "…within 30 days…" makes sense on its own card.

✅ Best for: large libraries where chunks like "it applies to returns" are ambiguous alone. ⚠️ Watch: extra tokens per chunk (and per dollar); Bedrock KB offers this as a newer option.
On AWS

How this shows up in Bedrock

In a typical AWS RAG stack: docs live in S3 → Bedrock Knowledge Bases parses, chunks (with your chosen strategy), and embeds them (Titan Embeddings or a partner model) → vectors land in a store like S3 Vectors, OpenSearch, or Aurora pgvector → queries retrieve top-k chunks and optionally rerank them before the model answers.

Two size rules to respect: chunks must fit the embedding model's input limit (e.g. Titan Embeddings v2 accepts ~8k tokens), and top-k × chunk size must fit the LLM's context window with room to spare. Bigger chunks = fewer top-k slots you can afford.

Fine print

Gotchas worth knowing

⚠️ Overlap isn't optional

Ideas that straddle a boundary get amputated without 10–20% overlap. The cost: some text is stored twice — that's the cheap insurance.

⚠️ Changing strategy = re-embedding everything

New chunks mean new vectors for the whole corpus. Treat a chunking change like a schema migration: test retrieval quality first, then commit.

⚠️ Tables hate being split

Naive chunking shreds a pricing table into meaningless rows. Keep tables whole, or extract them into descriptive sentences before embedding.

✅ Chunks need their address

"…within 30 days…" is useless alone. Prepend "Acme Policy > Returns > EU" and suddenly every card is self-contained. Cheap, high-impact fix.

✅ Small chunks + rerank is a power combo

Retrieve many small, precise chunks, then let Bedrock Rerank pick the best few to send to the LLM. Precision at search time, richness at prompt time.

✅ Evaluate, don't vibe

Build a small set of real questions with known source passages, and measure retrieval hit-rate per strategy. The best chunking is the one that wins your eval, not the trendiest one.

TL;DR

Explain it to me in four lines

  • Chunking = cutting your library into index cards. RAG quality is decided at the scissors, not at the model.
  • Start with Bedrock KB's default (recursive, ~300 tokens, ~20% overlap) — it's right for most prose.
  • Upgrade deliberately: layout-aware for tables/manuals, parent-child or semantic + rerank for precision, no chunking for tiny docs — and give every chunk its address (title + section path).
  • Re-cutting means re-embedding everything, so evaluate with real questions first — then commit.
