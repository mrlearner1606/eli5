🧠Two big words, one simple job

Your Gen AI app is precious. People will try to read its secrets, trick its model, or abuse its power. Two words describe how you stop that:

🔒 Security "Who gets in?" Guards at every door. Check IDs, lock the treasure chest, watch for sneaky visitors. It's protection.
📖 Governance "Who made the rules?" The rule book: who may use the AI, what it may never say, how much it may spend, and a diary proving you followed the rules. It's order.
🧸 In one breath: Security is the castle's walls and guards. Governance is the king's rule book that tells everyone what they may and may not do.

🧅One lock is not enough — use onion layers

Never guard your castle with a single wall. If one layer fails, the next layer catches the trouble. Cloud people call it defense in depth.

🧱Identity — who are you, really?IAM · Cognito
🚪Network — keep it inside the castleVPC · PrivateLink
🛡️Model safety — filter bad prompts & answersBedrock Guardrails
🔐Data — lock the treasure chestKMS · Macie
📜Watch & prove — the castle diaryCloudTrail · CloudWatch · Audit Manager
🧸 Even if a guard naps, the next wall is awake. Layers win.

🧰The AWS guard kit, at a glance

🪪 IAM

Hands out the least-privilege keys: roles, policies, and temporary credentials. Never hardcode keys in code!

🛡️ Bedrock Guardrails

A filter wall around any model — blocked topics, denied words, PII masking, jailbreak defense.

🔑 KMS

The master keymaker: encrypts your data and keeps keys under your control.

🚪 VPC & PrivateLink

Private corridors between your app and Bedrock — traffic never walks the open street.

📜 CloudTrail

The castle diary — records every API call so you can prove who did what, when.

🔍 Macie & Inspector

Sniffer dogs: Macie finds hidden private data in S3; Inspector hunts weak spots in your setup.

📋 AWS Config

The rules checker — alerts you when something drifts away from the approved plan.

🧭 Audit Manager

Auto-collects evidence so audits become a formality, not a fire drill.

📖Governance: the king's rule book

Security guards the doors; governance decides what the doors are for. For a Gen AI app that means four pages in the book:

RuleWhat it means
🧑‍🏫 Human in the loopA teacher checks the AI's homework before it goes out — especially for anything risky (health, money, legal).
🎭 Allowed usesWrite down what the AI may and may never do. Enforce it with Guardrails, not just good intentions.
💸 Cost guardrailsBudgets and alarms stop the AI from eating coins all night. Governance covers spending too!
🧾 Audit trailLogs, versions, and evidence you can show a grown-up (auditor) later. If it isn't written down, it didn't happen.
🧸 Security = guards. Governance = rule book + diary + budget jar. You need both.

⚠️Gotchas that bite real builders

  • 🤝 Shared responsibility. AWS locks the building; you lock your rooms. Running Bedrock doesn't secure your app or data.
  • 🗝️ Guardrails ≠ data security. A topic filter won't encrypt your S3 bucket. You need both walls and filters.
  • 🔑 Never hardcode keys. Secrets in code end up in git history. Use IAM roles and a secrets manager.
  • 🎭 Jailbreaks evolve. People find new tricks to trick models. Update guardrails and test them like code.
  • 🐛 Config drift. Someone "temporarily" opens a door and forgets. AWS Config catches the drift; re-check often.
  • 🙈 PII sneaks into prompts. Users paste private data. Mask it at the gate with Guardrails PII handling.

⭐TL;DR — the castle in six lines

  • Security guards the doors (who gets in); governance writes the rules (what may happen).
  • Use defense in depth — many onion layers, so one crack never sinks the castle.
  • Give every guard the one key they need: least-privilege IAM roles, never hardcoded secrets.
  • Filter both directions: prompts coming in and answers going out, with Bedrock Guardrails.
  • Lock data with KMS, keep traffic in private corridors, and write everything into the CloudTrail diary.
  • Governance adds human checks, budgets, and audit evidence — and it's a full exam domain (AIF-C01).
