The idea

A document is a giant puzzle. The AI has to look at it one piece at a time.

Imagine you give a gen-AI model a huge document — a whole book, a long contract, or two hundred customer emails.

The model can't hold the whole thing at once. It has a size limit for each conversation, called a context window. So the job is to split that big thing into smaller pieces the model can actually use.

Those pieces are chunks. And how you split them — that's the chunking strategy.

🧸 In one breath: Cut the giant page into chunks. Feed the right chunks to the model. Get answers that actually make sense.

Why it matters

Chunking decides what the model can remember.

If chunks are too big, you overflow the model or bury the answer inside a wall of text.

If chunks are too small, the model loses the surrounding sentence, table, or paragraph — and starts guessing.

If chunks are in the wrong place, you can cut through the middle of an idea and the model gets confused.

That's why chunking isn't "just split by character count." It's one of the biggest choices you make when building a RAG app or a Bedrock knowledge base.

How it works

The bite-sized pipeline

01 Bring in the document. PDFs, text files, logs, web pages, call transcripts — whatever your data is.
⬇
02 Split it into chunks using your chosen strategy — fixed size, sentences, paragraphs, or something smarter.
⬇
03 Turn each chunk into something the model can compare. Usually an embedding — a list of numbers that captures what the chunk is about. In AWS, this often happens in Amazon Bedrock or through Bedrock Data Automation.
⬇
04 Store the chunks and their numbers. In a knowledge base or vector store, so you can find the right pieces later.
⬇
05 Ask a question. Find the chunks that match, hand them to the model, and get your answer.

🧸 A kid-friendly version: Read the book → tear out bite-size pages → tag each page with what it's about → put them on labeled shelves → when a friend asks a question, grab the right pages and read them out loud.

The choices

Chunking strategies — how you decide where to cut.

Every chunking strategy is a different answer to the same question: where does one piece end and the next begin?

Fixed-size

Cut by length.

Split the text into chunks of a set number of characters or tokens, sometimes with overlap so nothing gets dropped at the seams.

Sentence / paragraph

Cut by meaning boundaries.

Keep sentences or paragraphs together so each chunk stays readable, instead of slicing words in half.

Semantic

Cut by topic.

Use the text itself — headings, breaks, or even embeddings — to find where one idea ends and another begins.

Parent-child / hierarchical

Small chunks, big context.

Keep a tiny chunk for precise search, but remember which bigger chunk it came from, so the model still gets the wider paragraph or page.

🧸 The main tradeoff: fixed-size is simple and predictable. Sentence/paragraph is cleaner to read. Semantic is smarter but more work. Parent-child gives you precision and context — usually the best of both worlds.

Tradeoffs

Too big vs. too small — the sweet spot in the middle.

😵 Too big
  • The chunk overflows what the model can handle.
  • The real answer hides inside a pile of extra text.
  • Search gets sloppy because one chunk covers too much.
✅ A thoughtful chunk
  • Fits the model's working memory.
  • Keeps the sentence, table, or idea intact.
  • Still carries enough context to make sense.
On AWS

Where this lives in the AWS Gen AI world.

📚 Amazon Bedrock Knowledge Bases

Manages document ingestion, chunking, and embeddings for RAG. You bring the data; Bedrock helps turn it into chunks the model can search through. Chunk size and strategy choices shape how well it finds the right answers.

🤖 Amazon Bedrock / Foundation Models

The model itself has a context window — the amount it can handle in one turn. Chunking is how you fit your data into that window without losing the meaning your app actually needs.

🧹 Amazon Bedrock Data Automation

A newer AWS service that can take messy files and produce structured, chunk-like outputs you can feed into a gen-AI workflow — useful when your data isn't clean text to begin with.

🧵 Amazon SageMaker

When you need full control, you can build your own chunking pipeline here — custom split logic, custom embeddings, custom storage — instead of relying on a managed service.

🧸 Plain talk: on AWS, chunking is the bridge between your raw documents and the model. Pick a managed path for speed, or build your own for control.

Why it's cool

Three reasons chunking makes Gen AI actually useful.

🧩 It fits.

Chunks let huge documents play nicely with models that can only hold a limited amount at a time.

🎯 It finds.

Good chunks make search smarter — you grab the right piece, not the whole book.

🧠 It remembers context.

Smart chunking — overlap, parent chunks, semantic breaks — keeps ideas whole so the model doesn't get confused.
